Overview

This course is designed to give students knowledge and tools to live a life of optimal wellness. Students will learn how wellness is a compilation of positive choices throughout the day that lead to a life of balance. The course will cover the seven areas of wellness: physical, emotional, mental, social, spiritual, intellectual, and environmental. In each area, the student will learn the principles associated with living well. They will have opportunities to incorporate the concepts into their own lives. Recognizing wellness-diminishing activities in everyday life and gaining the tools to overcome those challenges will be presented and students will learn positive lifestyle changes leading to the highest quality of life.

AI Policy

We are not interested in developing artificial intelligence (AI) in this class. We are focused on developing your intelligence, your critical thinking, and your teaching skills.

All assignment questions should be answered in your own words and show personalization. This course focuses on your personal health and wellness. Answers should be specific to you. We want to know of your ideas and personal experiences.

Avoid using AI tools to answer assignment questions. AI tools include, but are not limited to:

  • Grammarly
  • ChatGPT
  • Microsoft CoPilot (you have free access to this one through MS365)
  • Google Gemini
  • Lucid Collaborative AI
  • Adobe Firefly

Course Outcomes

  1. Identify personal wellness, skills, behaviors, and routines.
  2. Examine my current lifestyle as it relates to my personal wellness.
  3. Explore physical, mental, emotional, spiritual, social, and environmental wellness practices.
  4. Apply personal behaviors, habits, or patterns that promote optimal wellness.

Learning Model

The Learning Model is designed to help students become more involved in their learning through preparation, teaching others, and applying what they have learned.

You will be applying some of the outcomes, principles, and processes throughout this course (See the major assignments chart above.) You are encouraged to learn by study and also by faith (D&C 88:118). You will constantly take action in this course as you prepare through thoughtful study, engage in discussion with your classmates to teach one another, and ponder and prove your study by demonstrably applying the principles and topics in the course. You will also be invited to reflect how you can use what you have learned to increase your discipleship in Jesus Christ by becoming a leader in your home, church, and community.

Workload

You should plan to devote a significant amount of time each week to this course. Also, keep in mind that just like learning a new instrument, spending a little bit of time each day is better than a large amount of time in one sitting. If you put off the work of this course until Saturday, you will likely not be successful. If you start early, ask questions, and work diligently, you will be successful and develop the skills employers want.

Late Work

Turning work in on time is a sign of professionalism and respect for the instructor as well as the other students in the class. Students are expected to submit all work on time.

If you do not submit an assignment or graded quiz by the due date, that assignment or quiz will automatically be given a score of zero. Submitting work late may still earn you points, as long as it is within 7 days of the due date or before the end of the term in week 6-7. To prevent falling behind, it is best to submit your work on time or early.

Accommodations will be made for those who have difficulties submitting assignments on time. Contact your instructor if you need help.
